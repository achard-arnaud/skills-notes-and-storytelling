# Debrief 360

## Purpose

Turn a completed business call into an evidence-grounded executive debrief that improves both the business move and the participant's posture. Use preparation artifacts as the baseline, meeting evidence as the delta, and coaching as a separate interpretation layer.

## Input contract

Required:
- call objective and relationship context;
- participant being coached and target counterparty;
- at least one preparation artifact or explicit pre-call hypothesis;
- meeting notes, transcript or human recap;
- business decision or next-step question.

Optional:
- recording or timestamped transcript;
- CRM history and prior interactions;
- follow-up message or commitments;
- observer feedback;
- self-assessment from the coached participant.

If only automatic meeting notes exist, mark posture findings provisional. Do not infer interruption, tone, pace, body language, exact wording or speaking-time balance.

## Mandatory method

1. Resolve the canonical preparation baseline.
2. Create a delta ledger: confirmed, contradicted, newly learned, still unknown.
3. Build atomic meeting fragments and preserve speaker/source lineage.
4. Select one primary business framework using the router below; add at most one supporting framework.
5. Build a ghost deck with action titles before drafting.
6. Separate business debrief, posture debrief and next-move design.
7. Insert coaching side stories only after the decision spine is coherent.
8. Apply Pyramid Principle, MECE and signal-to-noise lint.
9. End with a bounded close: owner, action, evidence, metric, date and stop condition.

## Framework router

| Dominant decision | Primary framework | Typical output |
|---|---|---|
| Sequence core, growth engine and ambition | Three Horizons | H1 protect or monetize, H2 build, H3 option or north star |
| Choose markets, segments or bets | GE McKinsey portfolio matrix | attractiveness, strength, invest/select/harvest |
| Diagnose organization or execution coherence | McKinsey 7S | hard and soft alignment gaps |
| Drive behavioral adoption or transformation | McKinsey Influence Model | role model, understanding, skills, reinforcement |
| Problem remains ambiguous | MECE issue tree and hypothesis tree | decisive questions, evidence and falsifiers |

Use the smallest framework that changes the decision. Never name a framework merely to decorate the output.

## Required sections with action-title grammar

1. Answer first: business result and coaching priority.
2. What changed versus preparation.
3. Business diagnosis through the selected framework.
4. What the coached participant did that created value.
5. What reduced impact or left value on the table.
6. Counterparty signals and intention hypotheses.
7. C-level posture coaching with observable behavior, impact and alternative move.
8. Next move with message, questions, owner, metric and stop condition.
9. Ghost deck action titles.
10. Evidence boundaries and sources.

Render enumerations of three or more items as bullets. Use numbers only when order or priority changes meaning.

Each heading states the insight or action, not the topic.

## Side stories

Preferred coaching kinds:
- `posture_tip` for one reusable behavioral move;
- `strong_point` for a behavior to preserve and why it worked;
- `priority_highlight` for the one issue with disproportionate impact;
- `missed_opportunity` for a moment where another question or close would have improved the outcome;
- `latent_signal` for an intention hypothesis that remains explicitly testable.

Existing `dezoom`, `method`, `false_lead`, `comparator`, `analytical_focus` and `callback` remain available. Every side story requires claim lineage, an insertion anchor, a return anchor and an evidence status. A coaching side story may interpret evidence but cannot manufacture an event, quote or intention.

## Coaching evidence ladder

| Evidence | Allowed conclusion |
|---|---|
| Timestamped transcript or recording | Exact words, sequence and observable conversational move |
| Human notes with speaker attribution | Attributed content, not exact wording or tone |
| Automatic meeting summary | Themes and candidate actions; posture remains provisional |
| Preparation artifact only | Pre-call hypothesis, never post-call performance |

## Quality gates

- opening contains one answer, no chronology dump;
- business facts, interpretations, hypotheses and recommendations are distinguishable;
- preparation baseline and meeting delta are explicit;
- primary framework is justified by the decision question;
- every coaching point includes observed evidence, likely impact and an alternative behavior;
- counterparty intent is always a hypothesis unless explicitly stated;
- no confidential claim, client name or personal assessment is recommended for onward sharing without authorization;
- no more than three posture priorities;
- next step has a decision and stop condition, not a generic follow-up;
- automatic notes never support claims about tone, interruptions or speaking time.

## Default layout

Portrait, 5 to 8 pages. Use prose for the answer and coaching interpretation, a compact comparison table for evidence-to-impact patterns, and no more than one framework table per major section. Side stories remain short and return explicitly to the decision spine.
