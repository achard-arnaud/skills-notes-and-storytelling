# Shared editorial and research quality

This contract applies across internal skills and repository skills that produce analytical or decision-facing content. Read it from the relevant skill instead of copying the rules. Research budgets apply only when the task investigates a company or its business; they do not apply to document-only, pedagogical, rendering-only or non-company tasks.

## Direct, tangible prose

- Write the point as a direct affirmative statement. Give each sentence one analytical job.
- Replace compressed contrast, negation and disclaimer patterns with explicit evidence and meaning.
- When the analysis compares customer response with operating autonomy, write two separate findings: what customer evidence says about satisfaction, and what observed examples show about autonomy, oversight or intervention. Attribute each finding and state its evidence status.
- When evidence is absent, name the missing measure and its decision consequence. For example: “The company publishes no renewal or satisfaction data. Buyers therefore cannot verify retention or customer-perceived value from public evidence.”
- Explain the business value of every material inference: who receives value, how the offer creates and captures it, and which evidence supports that mechanism. A label such as “inference” does not replace the business explanation.
- Treat pricing and packaging as explicit business questions. Report published policy, observed pricing signals, or the exact gap and its commercial consequence.

Avoid sentences such as:
> Its value is judged by work delivered and accepted after review, not by autonomy alone.

Write the evidence directly:
> Public customer cases describe the work delivered and the acceptance criteria. They provide no satisfaction score. The documented workflow routes consequential outputs to a human reviewer; this shows supervised autonomy at that step.

Avoid empty methodological conclusions such as:
> The economic mechanisms are explicit inferences, without recommendations.

State the business mechanism and payoff:
> The subscription captures recurring revenue from continued access. Public evidence does not disclose renewal rates, so retention strength remains unmeasured.

## Substance and methodological notes

- Put analysis, interpretation, uncertainty and business implications in the main content where the reader can use them.
- Keep methodological reversibility, scope notes and evidence-status legends distinct from analysis of the subject.
- Use footnotes, source notes and the end of a card/container for concise source, date, status or symbol legends. Keep them illustrative and visually discreet.
- Put analytical comments, caveats that change meaning, and decision implications in the relevant body section. Do not hide a substantive verdict in a footer, caption or end-of-container note.
- A method can be reversible while the underlying conclusion remains substantive. State the conclusion and its evidence in the body; record the reversible method separately in the process record.

## Final repetition review

Run a dedicated editorial pass after sourcing and before delivery. Compare the title, thesis, KPI band, cards, tables, callouts, captions, footnotes and conclusion.

1. Build a short inventory of material claims and their canonical container.
2. Find exact and semantic repetitions across containers, including repeated numbers, explanations, caveats and conclusions.
3. Keep each claim once in its strongest location. Retain a second mention only when it performs a distinct reader function, and make that function clear.
4. Remove repeated copy and replace it with a forward link or a new implication.
5. Re-read the complete artifact at reader speed. Confirm that every container adds evidence, analysis or a useful transition.

Record the pass as complete in QA. The repetition review remains separate from factual verification and counter-perspective review.

## Company research budget

For a business analysis of a named company, record the research budget and actual coverage.

### Company website

1. Discover the company sitemap or sitemap index and record the number of unique canonical HTML URLs.
2. Crawl and inspect more than 50% of those URLs. Use page titles, metadata, page text and source links where available.
3. Document the numerator, denominator, crawl date, coverage percentage and excluded URLs. Exclude only duplicates, non-HTML assets, stale archives or pages outside the declared company/business scope; state each exclusion rule.
4. If the site provides no sitemap, record the discovery methods and use the navigable company site as the denominator. If access blocks the threshold, report the achieved coverage and blocker as a research limitation.

### Complementary research

- Run 20–200 distinct, targeted searches across relevant source classes. Count unique search questions, not tool calls or repeated queries.
- Start with at least 20 questions. Increase the budget toward 200 when the company, market or ownership structure is complex, material claims conflict, source coverage is weak, or triangulation remains incomplete.
- Cover company disclosures, customers/partners, competitors/substitutes, investors/ownership, market/regulatory context and material newsflow as relevant.
- Track query count, source classes, recency, contradictions and unresolved gaps. Deduplicate query intent; use each search to test a named question or claim.
- Stop when material claims have independent corroboration, important contradictions have an explicit status, sitemap coverage exceeds 50%, and further searches are unlikely to change the business reading. State a concrete access, source or time constraint when the run cannot meet the budget.

A search budget is a coverage standard, not a volume target. Relevance, evidence lineage and triangulation determine completion.

## Skill propagation review

When creating or editing a skill, perform a transverse mention review:

- Link this contract from each output-producing skill that uses shared analysis or prose rules.
- Add the company research budget only to skills that investigate companies or business markets.
- Add the final repetition review to skills that assemble multi-section, card-based or rendered outputs.
- Keep shared rules centralized here; local skills state applicability and stage ownership.
- Record skills reviewed, links added, scope decisions and remaining follow-up in the change or issue.

For staged business writing, the scaffold and drafting skill applies the direct-prose and business-value rules. The final review stage applies repetition, footer and research-coverage checks.
