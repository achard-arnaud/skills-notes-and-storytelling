# Business two-pager - descriptive analysis and strategy

## Purpose and boundary

Use this two-page format to help an executive understand a company, business unit, market-facing offer or strategic move. The business view describes how the entity creates, delivers and captures value before assessing a fit, recommendation or candidate. A hiring or candidate brief is a separate subject spine and is used only when explicitly requested.

The two-pager is a compressed decision artifact, not a company profile assembled from every available fact. Select evidence that explains the business model, strategic position and execution. State the cutoff date and distinguish public fact, company claim, inference, hypothesis, contradiction and unknown.

## Select the business subject

Declare one primary spine before drafting:

- company or business unit;
- company and product portfolio;
- market and competitive position;
- strategy or transformation;
- target and offer fit, only when requested;
- company and person, only when requested.

Do not infer company truth from a job advertisement, seller truth from a customer case, or business performance from a strategy statement.

## Page 1 - Company, business and strategic signals

Lead with a descriptive thesis, not a recommendation about a candidate or employer. Select applicable modules; omit unavailable or immaterial information and label unknowns instead of filling gaps.

### 1. Company identity and scale

Create a compact signalétique from verifiable sources:

- legal and operating footprint, headquarters and locations;
- employees, revenue, growth, margin, assets under management, customers or other relevant KPIs, with period, unit and source;
- CEO, CTO, business leadership and board only when their roles affect the business analysis;
- ownership, funding, acquisitions or capital structure where material.

Do not force a metric set that does not fit the company. Do not present estimates as reported data.

### 2. Positioning and value creation

Describe markets served, customer segments and geography; products, services, use cases and revenue logic; distribution, delivery and operating model; value chain, key capabilities, partners and dependencies; and customer, revenue or mission mix where the sources support a meaningful split.

Explain the mechanism: customer need -> offer/capability -> delivery -> value captured. A list of sectors or products is not a business model.

### 3. Organization or product-structure visual

Reserve one visual container for a structure or segmentation view only if it answers a reader question faster than prose. Candidate views include organization or leadership structure; business-unit / market / customer segmentation; product portfolio and use-case map; and value chain, partner ecosystem or revenue flow.

Use Schema Flow Master for this harvest. Choose the diagram from the question: organization chart or flowchart TD for reporting/structure; mindmap or a table for a non-causal product hierarchy; C4Context for an ecosystem boundary; flowchart TD for a value chain. Keep the diagram macro, source-addressable and editable. Use at most five nodes horizontally; split views when the reader's question changes. Mark inferred reporting lines, product groupings or value flows as hypotheses. Do not invent executives, ownership, causality or reporting relationships.

No diagram is required when a compact table or prose fully resolves the question.

### 4. Two-year newsflow and strategic highlights

Reserve a distinct, time-bounded container for material events from the prior 24 months, ending at the analysis cutoff. Select only events that changed or may change strategy, economics, capability, ownership or market access: leadership changes, funding, acquisitions, launches, exits, major customer/partner moves, restructuring, regulation or stated strategic pivots.

For every item, include date, event, evidence status and business implication. Separate announced intent from completed execution. Avoid a generic chronological news digest.

### 5. SWOT -> TOWS

Use evidence-backed SWOT only when it adds explanatory value. Strengths and weaknesses describe internal capabilities, economics, operating constraints and dependencies. Opportunities and threats describe external market, customer, competitor, regulatory and technology conditions.

Then translate the material intersections into TOWS options: SO, ST, WO and WT. Each option is a conditional strategic path or question, not an unsupported recommendation. Rank only where evidence supports the priority; otherwise show the critical trade-off and unknown.

## Page 2 - Strategy, products and execution

### 1. Strategy and execution

Show the stated strategic intent, the capabilities and investments required, operating changes, owners/timing where sourced, and observed progress. Separate announced ambition; funded or resourced initiative; execution evidence; measured outcome; and unresolved dependency or execution gap.

Use one mechanism chain where it clarifies how strategy becomes operating results. The diagram must distinguish current state from target state and hypothesis.

### 2. Products and use cases

Describe the product or service portfolio at the level needed to explain customer value and the business model. For each material offer, state customer/job, use case, delivery or channel, monetization evidence and maturity. Avoid feature inventories unless a feature changes differentiation, economics or adoption.

### 3. Competitive insights and moat

Compare only relevant alternatives at a common level: direct competitors, substitutes, internal build, channel partners or doing nothing. Normalize segment, geography, scope and time horizon before comparison.

Describe the potential moat as an evidenced mechanism, such as switching costs, proprietary distribution/data, scale economics, regulatory position, network effects, trust, specialized operating know-how or ecosystem control. Separate a claimed moat from demonstrated durability. Note erosion paths and counterevidence. Do not assign a moat score without a defined basis.

### 4. Takeaways

End with three to five ranked business takeaways that synthesize the pages. If a decision was requested, state the decision implication, conditions, proof needed and smallest reversible next step. Otherwise end with the central strategic tension and the evidence that would resolve it. Do not append a candidate recommendation by default.

## Binding and evidence rules

- Keep source ledger and claims separate from layout. Every KPI carries date, units, perimeter and source.
- Bind only modules supported by evidence. Missing fields collapse cleanly; they do not produce empty cards or filler text.
- Preserve competing explanations and material unknowns through compression.
- Keep seller, company, customer, market and candidate evidence distinct until the relevant comparison stage.
- Use the business reference as the flexible authoring contract: prioritize the strongest business explanation. The transferred renderer retains a fixed outer frame, so map modules into its cards/columns and reorder content only where the JSON contract permits. Keep the two-page count, business-first thesis, distinct prior-24-month newsflow container and closing takeaways stable.
